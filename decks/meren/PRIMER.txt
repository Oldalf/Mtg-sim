This deck is a Golgari reanimator deck built for bracket 2 to 3.

The goal is a deck that is consistent and fun to play: slower than a combo deck, with a board and big drains instead of a one-turn kill. Meren and the other reanimation cards are value engines that swing the tempo, and forced sacrifice keeps the board under control.

HOW IT WORKS
Meren gets an experience counter whenever another creature you control dies. At your end step she returns a creature card from your graveyard to the battlefield if its mana value is at most your experience, and to your hand if not. So the deck fills the graveyard (self-mill and tutors), sacrifices creatures for value, and lets Meren bring back the best ones turn after turn.

WIN LINES
- Gray Merchant of Asphodel: each opponent loses life equal to your devotion to black. With 5 experience (its mana value) and a free sacrifice outlet, sacrifice it at your end step and Meren brings it back for another drain every turn.
- Lotleth Giant: damage to one opponent equal to the number of creature cards in your graveyard.
- Twilight Diviner doubles both, because a creature that enters from a graveyard makes a token copy. Saw in Half on your own Gray Merchant gives two copies.
- Grave Titan, Avenger of the Fallen and the sacrifice outlets (Viscera Seer, Woe Strider, Umbral Collar Zealot, Wight of the Reliquary) build the board and the experience.

FILLING THE GRAVEYARD AND FINDING PIECES
- Mill: Breach the Multiverse, Grisly Salvage, Grist, Liliana the Repentant, Satyr Wayfinder.
- Tutors: Buried Alive, Fauna Shaman, Grim Servant, Dina's Guidance, Eldritch Evolution, Vile Entomber, Protean Hulk, Rune-Scarred Demon.
- Ramp: Sol Ring, Llanowar Elves, Sakura-Tribe Elder, Solemn Simulacrum, Dark Ritual, Studious First-Year.
- Green is the colour of the ramp, so a Forest early matters.

CARE WITH
- Spore Frog is a fog. Keep it home, attack with the rest, and sacrifice it on their turn to prevent the damage coming back.
- Your own life matters. Phyrexian Arena, Infernal Grasp and Diresight cost life, and Gray Merchant and Disciple of Bolas gain it.

MULLIGANS
The first mulligan is free in my pod (a new 7), then it is a London mulligan (draw 7, bottom the extras). From simulating thousands of opening hands:
- Keep 3 to 5 lands if one of them makes green. Keep 2 lands only with a green source and a ramp piece, a graveyard filler or a tutor. No green source: mulligan.
- Mulligan 0 or 1 lands; a one-lander is worse than a six-card hand. Mulligan 7 lands. 6 lands is a mulligan the first time and a keep after that.
- A green source is the most important single thing, worth about two thirds of a turn of speed: green is the colour of the ramp. Having both black and green in the opening hand is nearly as good.
- The best cards to see early are Sol Ring, Liliana the Repentant, Sakura-Tribe Elder, Grist, Priest of Forgotten Gods, Fauna Shaman, Satyr Wayfinder and Grisly Salvage. A ramp piece or a graveyard filler or tutor is worth about a third of a turn each, so a hand with green and one of them is a keep, even with only 2 lands.
- Expensive payoffs (Gray Merchant, Lotleth Giant, Grave Titan) and sacrifice outlets don't speed the deck up by themselves. Don't keep a 2-land hand just because it has them.
- Removal and the two Witnesses look weak in a simulation only because the opponents there do nothing. In a real game a hand with removal is a fine keep.
